\begin{abstract}
Which physical priors make a small, local neural emulator competitive with a global
neural operator? We answer this with a controlled JAX benchmark of fourteen
architectures on ten two-dimensional and six three-dimensional PDE phenomena, run to
completion on a single GPU: five seeds per cell for the full matrix and ten for three
regime representatives, with every comparison reported as a paired Wilcoxon test on
shared initial conditions under Holm--Bonferroni correction. Three controls decide how
the numbers may be read---a do-nothing identity emulator, physics-free CNN and U-Net
surrogates at full and parameter-matched size, and the reference solver with its own
discretization error measured against closed-form solutions. No architecture wins
everywhere. Global spectral mixing leads on smooth diffusion, transport, and turbulent
flow; a bounded conservative cellular automaton leads by a wide margin on stiff
fourth-order dynamics ($\relerr=0.357$ against $0.650$ for the Fourier neural operator
at ten seeds); a purely conservative flux update leads on the multi-field conservation law;
and physics-free convolutional baselines lead on three of ten phenomena, which is
itself a result. Matched-width ablations show the effect of a conservation head
reversing sign between heat ($4.6\times$ better) and Nagumo ($24\times$ worse), and
output bounding cutting Cahn--Hilliard error by $30\%$ while reducing mass drift by
five orders of magnitude. We also report where the evidence stops: on heat the leading
model has reached the teacher's own discretization error, and four of nine scale sweeps
change the ranking, so every ordering here is quoted with the scale at which it was
measured.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
A neural PDE emulator must do more than predict the next field: it must keep making
useful predictions after its own errors become part of the input. Architecture
selection is therefore a question about the interaction between a learned update,
the governing dynamics, and the rollout horizon. Global neural operators offer
long-range communication, while local neural cellular automata offer compact,
translation-equivariant update rules \citep{li2020fno,mordvintsev2020growing}.
Neither design alone specifies which physical quantities should be conserved, which
states should be admissible, or how errors should be controlled over time.

This paper asks: \emph{when does a local, structure-preserving update provide a better
accuracy--resource trade-off than global mixing, and when does that same prior become
a liability?} The question connects representation learning to numerical modeling.
A conservation constraint removes entire classes of possible updates; this can reduce
learning burden when the constraint is correct, but exclude the target dynamics when
it is not. A wider receptive field expands communication without ensuring stable
integration. Likewise, conserving a scalar total does not imply reproducing the
underlying trajectory.

\paragraph{The falsifiable hypothesis.} We state it so that it can fail.
\emph{The best-performing structural prior is determined by the equation's structure,
not by the architecture's capacity: (i) where the dynamics are local and the quantity
conserved, a local conservative update is competitive with a global spectral operator
at a fraction of the parameters; (ii) where the solution is globally coupled or carries
fine-scale structure, global mixing wins; (iii) where the dynamics are
non-conservative, imposing conservation costs accuracy.} Each clause is falsified by a
paired test on shared initial conditions showing the opposite ordering, or no
resolvable difference. We report both outcomes, and clause~(i) survives only in its
weaker, resource-relative form.

\paragraph{Why the controls decide the reading.}
An architecture comparison is only as informative as what it is measured against, and
three baselines are absent from most such comparisons. We include all three, and each
changes a conclusion. A \textbf{do-nothing identity emulator} $g(x)=x$ makes ``learned
worse than nothing'' a reportable outcome; at ten seeds the plain NCA is on the wrong
side of it on Navier--Stokes. \textbf{Physics-free ResNet and U-Net surrogates}, at full
size and at parameter counts matched to the cellular automata, prevent a conservation
result from being confounded with never having tried an ordinary convolutional network;
they win three of ten phenomena outright. The \textbf{reference solver}, with its own
error measured against closed-form solutions where they exist, bounds what any
distillation-trained model can mean: on heat the best architecture has already reached
it.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A regime map with uncertainty.} Fourteen architectures on ten 2-D and six
3-D phenomena under one distillation harness, at five seeds per cell and ten for the
three regime representatives, with bootstrap intervals and Holm-corrected paired tests
on shared initial conditions (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\item \textbf{Mechanism-focused ablations that reverse.} The effect of a conservation
head changes sign between a conservative and a non-conservative equation at matched
backbone and parameter count, and output bounding on stiff dynamics buys accuracy and
five orders of magnitude of mass drift simultaneously (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).
\item \textbf{An accounting of what the comparison is worth.} We measure the teacher's
own discretization error, split into spatial and temporal parts; we report the
amortization crossover against a per-instance PINN; and we test whether the ranking
survives changes of grid, horizon, and training budget. It does not always
(Sections~\ref{sec:teachererr}--\ref{sec:scaling}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Neural cellular automata.}
NCAs learn a single homogeneous local update applied identically at every cell
\citep{mordvintsev2020growing}. \citet{richardson2024patterns} train NCAs on PDE
trajectories, demonstrate generalization beyond the training data, and constrain them
to respect symmetries. \citet{saha2026timestepping} propose an NCA surrogate for PDE
time-stepping with the same motivation used here---a small, local, homogeneous rule
mirroring the locality of differential operators---benchmarked against PDE-Net, a PINN,
and an FNO on five canonical equations beyond the training horizon. That NCAs are viable
autoregressive PDE surrogates, and that they can be structurally constrained, is
therefore established rather than claimed here. Our question is narrower: whether the conservation prior still pays once the
update is \emph{also} required to be a single shared local rule, and on which
equations.

\paragraph{Learned local discretizations and graph solvers.}
PDE-Net constrains filters to approximate differential operators
\citep{long2018pdenet}, and learned data-driven discretizations replace hand-derived
stencils with fitted ones \citep{barsinai2019discretizations}. Message-passing and
mesh-based simulators generalize over
geometry and resolution \citep{brandstetter2022message,pfaff2021mesh,%
sanchez2020simulation}, at the cost of graph construction that a periodic regular grid does
not require; under-reaching in such solvers is a known failure mode
\citep{tesan2025underreaching}. Our domains are periodic and regular, so these methods
are discussed rather than benchmarked.

\paragraph{Conservation by construction and by correction.}
Finite-volume bookkeeping makes conservation a property of the update rather than of
the loss \citep{leveque2002finite}. FINN embeds that structure so each quantity follows
its own conservation law with explicit inter-cell fluxes
\citep{praditia2021finn,karlbauer2022composing}, and Neural Conservation Laws
parameterize networks satisfying the continuity equation exactly
\citep{richterpowell2022conservation}. Related work enforces constraints by projection or
correction \citep{hansen2023probconserv,liu2024clawno}. \emph{A conservative flux-form
neural PDE model is therefore not new}, and we do not claim it. What we contribute on
this axis is a controlled measurement of when that prior helps once it is imposed
inside a shared CA update, and a bound-preserving way to restore mass after clipping.

\paragraph{Neural operators and residual-based learning.}
The FNO learns a resolution-flexible global operator \citep{li2020fno,neuraloperator};
DeepONet learns a map between function spaces from operator samples
\citep{lu2021deeponet}; PINO adds a residual term \citep{li2021pino}, and
physics-informed DeepONet couples operator learning with physics constraints
\citep{wang2021pideeponet}. PINNs solve one initial-value problem per training run
\citep{raissi2019pinn}, with known optimization failure modes, causality, and
spectral-bias pathologies
\citep{krishnapriyan2021failure,wang2024causality,rahaman2019spectral}. We measure a PINN
under a matched task rather than in the same accuracy column
(Section~\ref{sec:matched}); PINO is not measured and is named as the clearest omission.

\paragraph{Long-horizon learning and frequency-resolved error.}
Autoregressive rollout error is a distinct failure mode from one-step accuracy
\citep{lippe2023refiner,um2020solver}. Dilated stencils widen context without
downsampling \citep{yu2016dilated}. We report frequency-resolved residuals and error
growth alongside aggregate error for this reason.

\paragraph{Benchmarking and scope.}
Benchmark design and reporting bias in scientific ML are themselves active concerns
\citep{mcgreivy2024baselines,pdebench,apebench}. We follow that
literature by including a do-nothing floor, physics-free controls, and the numerical
solver's own cost and error in every comparison.

\section{Problem and Experimental Design}
\label{sec:problem}
\subsection{Teacher emulation, not interchangeable learning tasks}
Let $\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}$ denote a numerical teacher for
$\partial_t u=\mathcal{N}[u;\theta_{\mathrm{phys}}]$ on a periodic grid.
An emulator $g_\theta$ approximates the discrete map
$u_t\mapsto\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}(u_t)$ and is evaluated recursively from held-out
initial conditions. Unless otherwise stated, $s_t$ denotes its grid state, with
physical fields represented as channels. The target is the \emph{teacher's discrete
trajectory}, not a verified continuum solution. The distinction matters when the
teacher contains a stabilizing operation such as clipping, and
Section~\ref{sec:teachererr} quantifies how much it matters on each equation.

The main suite contains ten 2-D phenomena: heat, advection--diffusion, Allen--Cahn,
wave, shallow-water, Nagumo, FitzHugh--Nagumo (FHN), Navier--Stokes (NS),
Cahn--Hilliard (CH), and Gray--Scott. Six are also evaluated in 3-D. Equations and
implementation details are in Appendix~\ref{sec:equations}. A PINN fitted to individual
initial-value problems is compared under a matched task in
Section~\ref{sec:matched} rather than pooled into the emulator ranking.

\subsection{Shared optimization and one protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}
Within a comparison, emulators share the teacher, initial conditions, optimizer
(AdamW), and rollout-MSE objective. For a training horizon $K$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_K(\theta)=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{\tau=1}^{K}
\operatorname{MSE}\!\left(g_\theta^{\circ\tau}(s_0),
\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\circ\tau}(s_0)\right).
\label{eq:training}
\end{equation}
Gradients pass through the model trajectory using backpropagation through time.
All 2-D results use one protocol: a $48^2$ grid, batch 32, 12 training steps, 48
evaluation steps, 1{,}200 epochs, He/Kaiming-normal initialization before ReLU,
zero-initialized update heads, learning-rate warmup, and teacher pre-evolution of
initial conditions (``pre-seeding''), with \model{preseed\_steps=0} for CH, whose
solutions coarsen too quickly from fresh initial conditions for developed states to
match the evaluation distribution. The full matrix uses \textbf{five seeds} per cell and
the three regime representatives---heat, CH, and NS---use \textbf{ten}. The 3-D suite
uses $24^3$, 600 epochs, and a single seed, and is labeled as such wherever it appears.
These shared settings equalize data and objective; they do not equalize parameter
counts, FLOPs, or tuning budgets, which is why Section~\ref{sec:efficiency} groups
results by parameter budget rather than ranking across budgets.

\paragraph{Statistics.}
Every architecture is evaluated on the \emph{same} held-out initial conditions in the
same order, so comparisons are paired. Reported errors are per-initial-condition
relative $L^2$, summarized with a 10{,}000-sample percentile bootstrap. Architecture
comparisons are paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Holm--Bonferroni correction
across the thirteen comparisons made per equation. A difference is called significant
only when the bootstrap interval on the paired differences excludes zero \emph{and}
Wilcoxon rejects; otherwise it is reported as a tie, meaning unresolvable at this
sample size rather than equal. At the headline sample size ($n=80$ paired evaluations)
no difference in Table~\ref{tab:heatms} is a tie.

\paragraph{Measurement.}
The diagnostic panel also includes pointwise error, PSNR, high-frequency error fraction,
error growth, per-channel mass drift, boundary residual, gradient energy, parameter
count, training wall-clock, and inference latency (Appendix~\ref{sec:metrics}). Mass
drift measures change from initial mass; it is an invariant error only for a quantity
the target PDE actually conserves, so a low drift score on a reaction system indicates a
wrong constraint rather than fidelity.

\paragraph{Compute and provenance.}
All results reported here were produced by one unattended 22.2-hour run on a single
NVIDIA RTX 4090, preceded by a 205-test correctness gate that asserts the JAX ports
equal verbatim PyTorch references to tolerance. Every results file records the JAX
version, backend, devices, and exact configuration. Six stages of that run
failed---three out-of-distribution studies and three stability studies---and one,
resolution transfer, was skipped; those axes are therefore reported as \emph{not
measured} at this scale rather than filled in from earlier reduced-scale runs
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\section{Structure-Aligned Update Rules}
\label{sec:methods}
\label{sec:arch}
The model family separates three operations: neighborhood perception, learned
update generation, and structural correction. The plain NCA uses a learned circular
$3\times3$ perception convolution $P_\theta$, followed by a pointwise MLP:
\begin{equation}
 s_{t+1}=s_t+f_\theta(s_t),\qquad
 f_\theta(s_t)=\operatorname{MLP}_\theta(\operatorname{ReLU}(P_\theta(s_t))).
\label{eq:plain}
\end{equation}
The residual head is unconstrained. Local computation is inexpensive, but cannot
encode an arbitrary global dependency in one update.

\input{table-axes}

\subsection{Conservation as a restriction on the hypothesis class}
The conservative \model{DeepFluxNCA}, abbreviated PI-NCA, predicts a flux along
each spatial axis and applies a periodic discrete divergence:
\begin{equation}
 s_{t+1}=s_t+D_hF_\theta(s_t),\qquad
 D_hF=\sum_{a=1}^{d}\left(\operatorname{roll}_a(F_a)-F_a\right).
\label{eq:flux}
\end{equation}
For each conserved field, periodic shifts preserve sums, so
$\sum_{\mathrm{cells}}D_hF=0$ and the discrete total is unchanged in exact
arithmetic, as in conservative finite-volume bookkeeping
\citep{leveque2002finite}. This holds for \emph{any} weights, at initialization and
after training, which is what distinguishes it from a penalty. It does not constrain
the accuracy of the spatial redistribution or guarantee energy dissipation. Here
``physics-informed'' refers to architectural conservation: these NCAs are trained on
teacher data, not solely on a PINN-style residual loss.

\model{MultiChannelFluxNCA} predicts a separate flux for each field ($2C$ flux
channels in 2-D), so each field's total is conserved separately. Lumping the channels
into a single total would let one field's deficit be paid out of another's, which is
wrong for any multi-field system. It is appropriate for source-free periodic
conservation laws in conserved variables, such as shallow-water mass and momentum.
Applying the same constraint to both FHN reaction fields deliberately tests a
mismatched prior.

\subsection{Spatial reach and admissible states}
\model{MultiScaleFluxNCA} concatenates perception features at dilations 1, 2, and 4
before predicting conservative fluxes. These perception stencils reach $\pm4$
cells rather than $\pm1$; the final divergence can enlarge the end-to-end dependency
further. The hybrid remains spatially local, but broadens communication at modest
parameter cost, following the established mechanism of expanding context without
downsampling \citep{yu2016dilated}.
\model{SpectralFluxNCA} instead combines a local flux stream with
a global spectral residual and a mass-restoring correction:
\begin{equation}
 s_{t+1}=\mathcal{R}_{M_t}\!\left(s_t+D_hF_\theta(s_t)
                       +g_\theta^{\mathrm{spectral}}(s_t)\right),
 \qquad M_t=\sum_{\mathrm{cells}}s_t.
\label{eq:spectral}
\end{equation}
The restoration is important: adding an unrestricted spectral residual would
otherwise remove the conservation guarantee.

For stiff bounded fields, \model{BoundedConsFluxNCA} clips the candidate state and then
restores the recorded total:
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde s=\operatorname{clip}(s_t+D_hF_\theta(s_t),\ell,h),
 \qquad s_{t+1}=\mathcal{R}_{M_t}(\widetilde s).
\label{eq:bounded}
\end{equation}
The restored quantity is \emph{mass}, not the CH free energy, and the composition is
not sound for the obvious choice of $\mathcal{R}$. A uniform offset
$\delta=(M_t-\sum\widetilde s)/N$ moves every cell, including those the clip has just
pinned to a bound, so the update is then neither bounded nor usefully both. We
therefore distribute the deficit in proportion to each cell's remaining headroom,
$r_i=h-\widetilde s_i$ when mass must be added and $\widetilde s_i-\ell$ when it must be
removed:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{R}_{M_t}(\widetilde s)_i=\widetilde s_i
 +\operatorname{sign}(d)\min\!\Big(\tfrac{|d|}{C},1\Big)\,r_i,
 \qquad d=M_t-\textstyle\sum_j\widetilde s_j,\quad C=\textstyle\sum_j r_j.
\label{eq:headroom}
\end{equation}
Mass is restored exactly whenever the target is feasible for the box, no cell can cross
the bound because the total increment is capped by the total headroom by construction,
the map is differentiable, and it is one vectorized pass. When
$M_t\notin[N\ell,Nh]$ the field saturates and the residual mass error is reported rather
than paid for by violating the bound. Ablation A7 measures what the naive choice costs
instead of asserting that the fix is free; the bound $[\ell,h]$ is measured from a short
teacher rollout rather than hardcoded, and is not an automatic maximum-principle
guarantee for the continuum equation.

The FNO baseline uses Fourier-space mixing plus pointwise maps
\citep{li2020fno}. Its global receptive field is useful for long-range coupling but
carries a larger parameter budget in the standard configuration. The physics-free
controls---a residual CNN with circular padding and a U-Net with mean-pool
downsampling---use zero-initialized heads, so like the NCAs they begin as the identity
map. Architecture schematics are retained in
Appendix~\ref{sec:architecture-details}.

\section{Results: Which Prior Helps Which Dynamics?}
\label{sec:results}
\subsection{A regime map, not a universal winner}
Table~\ref{tab:regime2d} summarizes the ten 2-D phenomena. Every winner beats the
identity floor, so on no equation is the comparison a ranking of noise. The winners
divide into four groups, and only two of them are the ones the hypothesis anticipated.

\input{table-regime2d}

\paragraph{Global mixing leads on smooth, transported, and turbulent dynamics.}
The FNO is best on heat ($0.00157$), advection--diffusion ($0.00123$), and
Navier--Stokes ($0.0730$). On NS this is expected: velocity is reconstructed through a
globally coupled Poisson solve, and a short-range update must communicate over repeated
steps. On heat it is \emph{not} what a reduced-scale study of this benchmark previously
reported, and Section~\ref{sec:scaling} identifies why.

\paragraph{Conservation leads exactly where the quantity is conserved.}
On shallow water---three fields, source-free, genuinely conservative---the purely
conservative \model{pi\_nca} is best at $0.00709$ with 5{,}280 parameters, ahead of the
FNO's $0.00869$ with 592{,}995. Its mass drift is $8.5\times10^{-5}$ against the FNO's
$2.09$. This is clause~(i) of the hypothesis holding in the setting it describes.

\paragraph{Bounding leads on stiff fourth-order dynamics, decisively.}
At ten seeds the bounded conservative hybrid reaches $0.357$ on CH against $0.650$ for
the FNO, which places the largest model in the study twelfth of fourteen. The two bounded
variants occupy the top two positions and the margin over the third
($\model{resnet}$, $0.465$) is Holm-significant at $n=80$. The five-seed matrix agrees
($0.353$ against $0.654$, same ordering). Stiffness and a hard state range, not locality
as such, are what this prior is for.

\paragraph{Physics-free baselines lead on three of ten, which is a result.}
The U-Net is best on wave, Gray--Scott, and Nagumo. On Nagumo the entire top three are
physics-free (\model{unet} $0.00751$, \model{resnet} $0.0104$, \model{resnet\_iso}
$0.0109$) and the best conservative model is far behind. We report this prominently
because the same data without those controls would have shown a conservative NCA
winning a field of conservative NCAs.

\input{table-heatms}

\paragraph{Heat: the local models are close, not ahead.}
Table~\ref{tab:heatms} gives the full ten-seed ranking. The FNO leads at
$0.001447$; the best local conservative model, \model{multiscale\_flux\_nca}, is
$0.003699$ with $107\times$ fewer parameters. At this sample size the gap is
significant, so the accuracy claim runs the other way from the resource claim. The
defensible statement is the resource-relative one: a $5{,}520$-parameter local update
reaches $2.6\times$ the error of a $592{,}897$-parameter spectral operator. It is not
that the local model is more accurate.

\paragraph{Learning worse than nothing.}
On NS the plain NCA scores $0.528$ against the identity floor's $0.513$ at ten seeds,
and $0.492$ against $0.450$ at five: the only architecture--phenomenon pair in the study
on the wrong side of its floor, in both tables. An unconstrained local residual head,
trained through a rollout on globally coupled dynamics, is actively harmful rather than
merely weak.

\input{intervention-figure}

\subsection{Controlled interventions}
\label{sec:ablation}
A regime map is a correlation until the priors are switched independently at matched
capacity. Table~\ref{tab:ablsummary} reports four such interventions, each changing
exactly one thing.

\input{table-ablsummary}

\paragraph{A4: the value of conservation reverses with the equation.}
At matched backbone and parameter counts within $1\%$, replacing an unconstrained
residual head with a conservative flux head improves heat by $4.6\times$
($0.00743$ versus $0.0339$) and worsens Nagumo by $24\times$ ($0.379$ versus
$0.0161$). The mass column shows why the constraint is not free: the flux head holds
the total to $10^{-5}$ on \emph{both} equations, which is correct physics on the first
and an actively wrong prior on the second, where the true dynamics create and destroy
the quantity and the residual head's drift of order $10^{2}$--$10^{3}$ is the right
behavior rather than a defect. This is the strongest controlled evidence in the paper
for clauses~(i) and~(iii).

\paragraph{A5: spatial reach helps on both equations at this grid.}
Widening the stencil from $3\times3$ to $5\times5$ to dilated $(1,2,4)$ improves heat
monotonically ($0.00896\to0.00584\to0.00412$) and NS likewise
($0.679\to0.667\to0.583$). At a $24^2$ grid the heat ordering is the reverse, with the
smallest stencil winning; the number of steps a local rule needs to cross the domain
grows with the grid, so its optimal reach does too. The local models' disadvantage on
globally coupled dynamics is therefore a receptive-field problem, addressable without a
spectral transform, rather than an argument against locality as such.

\paragraph{A1 and A7: bounding buys accuracy, and the projection buys conservation.}
On CH, bounding the output reduces error from $0.515$ to $0.358$ and mass drift from
$19.9$ to $5.2\times10^{-5}$ at an identical $5{,}520$-parameter backbone; on
Allen--Cahn, whose states do not reach the bound, it changes nothing to four
significant figures. A7 then separates the two mechanisms. Accuracy is indistinguishable
across clip-only, uniform projection, and headroom projection ($0.3589$, $0.3614$,
$0.3583$), but mass drift is $18.6$, $1.07\times10^{-4}$, and $5.40\times10^{-5}$: the
clip provides the stability, the projection provides the conservation, and the
headroom form of Equation~\ref{eq:headroom} improves on the uniform one by a further
factor of two while additionally guaranteeing the bound it restores mass into. We
present it as a correctness guarantee that costs no accuracy, not as an accuracy gain.

\subsection{Is the distillation target meaningful?}
\label{sec:teachererr}
The emulators are trained to imitate a solver and scored against that same solver, so a
low error could mean accurate physics or a faithful copy of a poor approximation. We
measure the difference. For the linear constant-coefficient members the periodic problem
is diagonal in Fourier space and the continuum solution is available in closed form; for
the rest the timestep is refined and Cauchy differences bound the teacher's own error.

\input{table-teacher}

Every teacher converges, with observed orders of $0.94$--$1.03$ where forward Euler is
used. The consequence for the paper is specific and constraining: the teacher's error on
heat is $0.00114$ and the FNO's is $0.001447$, so the leading architecture has
\emph{saturated the target}. Differences below roughly $10^{-3}$ on heat are measuring
the solver's discretization error, not model quality, and the top of
Table~\ref{tab:heatms} should be read accordingly. On CH the teacher error is $0.0441$
against a best model error of $0.353$, an order of magnitude of headroom, so that
comparison is safely learning-limited.

\subsection{Does the ranking survive a change of scale?}
\label{sec:scaling}
Every table above is measured at one grid, one horizon, and one training budget. We
sweep each axis with everything else fixed and report Kendall's $\tau$ between the
ordering at the smallest and largest setting.

\input{table-scaling}

Four of nine sweeps change the ordering and two change the winner. Most consequentially,
on heat the winner changes from \model{multiscale\_flux\_nca} at a $24^2$ grid to
\model{fno} at $48^2$ ($\tau=0.73$). An earlier reduced-scale study of this same
benchmark reported the local conservative model as both more accurate and $107\times$
smaller than the FNO on heat; the accuracy half of that claim does not survive the
change of grid, and we withdraw it. The mechanism is the one A5 exhibits: a local rule's
per-step receptive field is fixed in cells, so its advantage on a small domain is partly
an artifact of the domain being small. CH is the least stable phenomenon, with all three
axes changing the ordering. Every ranking in this paper is therefore quoted with its
operating point.

\subsection{What is it worth?}
\label{sec:matched}
Two comparisons that are usually omitted, both of which cut against the surrogates. A
PINN solves one initial-value problem per training run while an emulator trains once and
applies to any initial condition, so comparing them on a single accuracy column compares
different quantities. We fix the task instead.

\input{table-matched}

The emulator is better on $100\%$ of the 32 initial conditions
($p=4.7\times10^{-10}$) while receiving $431\times$ \emph{less} total wall-clock, so its
advantage is not bought with compute, and the amortization crossover is $K=1$. But the
row that matters is the third. At a $32^2$ grid the reference solver produces the exact
answer in $18.9$\,ms per initial condition, roughly a third of the emulator's $55$\,ms,
so \textbf{at this scale no surrogate in this paper is a deployment case}. At the
benchmark's own $48^2$ grid the gap closes to near parity and the emulator is faster on
two of ten phenomena (Table~\ref{tab:teacher}), which locates rather than removes the
problem: learned surrogates pay off where the solver does not fit---much larger grids,
much longer horizons, stiff timestep restrictions, or differentiability through an
entire rollout---and none of those conditions holds here.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
\label{sec:limitations}
The practical takeaway is a sequence of checks rather than an architecture. First
identify the target's actual invariants; A4 shows that imposing the wrong one costs a
factor of $24$. Then ask whether the model can communicate over the relevant spatial
range, remembering from A5 and Section~\ref{sec:scaling} that the answer depends on the
grid. Then ask whether the state has a hard range, since on stiff bounded dynamics
bounding was worth more than every other structural choice combined. Only then compare
accuracy and resources, and do so within a parameter budget. The hybrid designs are
candidate ways to compose these priors, not a validated selection algorithm.

Several limitations bound the conclusions, and three are consequences of this run rather
than of the design.

\textbf{Three evaluation axes are not measured at this scale.} The out-of-distribution
study (held-out amplitude, structure count, length scale, spectrum, PDE coefficient, and
horizon) and the guard-disabled long-horizon stability study each failed on all three
phenomena in this run, and the resolution-transfer sweep was skipped. Reduced-scale
results for these axes exist from earlier runs on different hardware, but mixing
backends within one study is exactly the error this paper's controls are meant to
prevent, so they are omitted rather than reported. The generated claims audit accompanying
the artifact marks these axes \textsc{not\_yet\_measured}.

\textbf{The heat comparison is teacher-limited at the top.} Section~\ref{sec:teachererr}
shows the leading model has reached the solver's own discretization error, so the
ordering among the leading entries of Table~\ref{tab:heatms} is not a clean measurement
of model quality.

\textbf{Rankings are conditional on scale.} Four of nine sweeps change the ordering.
Any use of Table~\ref{tab:regime2d} outside its operating point is unsupported, and a
previously reported heat result from this benchmark is withdrawn on exactly these
grounds.

Beyond these: the 3-D suite is single-seed and lacks the controls; PINO is not measured;
all domains are periodic and regular, so no claim extends to irregular geometry; the
solvers are our own, so numbers are not comparable to published PDEBench or APEBench
results; and teacher agreement establishes implementation consistency, not continuum
accuracy. The bound $[\ell,h]$ is measured from the teacher, and
Equation~\ref{eq:headroom} guarantees bounds and mass only when the target mass is
feasible for the box; infeasibility is reported, not hidden.

\section{Conclusion}
Locality is neither a universal advantage nor an intrinsic obstacle to PDE emulation. In
these experiments the useful prior is set by the equation: structural conservation wins
where the quantity is conserved and costs a factor of $24$ where it is not, output
bounding is worth more than every other structural choice on stiff bounded dynamics,
global mixing wins on smooth, transported, and turbulent problems, and a physics-free
convolutional baseline wins on three of ten phenomena. The contribution is a controlled account of these interactions together with
the machinery that makes the negative results reportable---an identity floor, physics-free
controls, paired statistics, the teacher's own error, and a rank-stability test that
withdrew one of our own earlier claims. Physical structure becomes a testable design
choice rather than a blanket argument for one model family.

\section*{Ethics Statement}
The reported study concerns numerical PDE benchmarks rather than human-subject
research. Its principal foreseeable risk is overinterpreting neural surrogates as
validated scientific simulators. We therefore distinguish teacher emulation from
continuum accuracy, report the solver's own error and cost alongside every model, and
state which evaluation axes were not measured. Deployment in safety-critical simulation
would require application-specific validation beyond this study.
