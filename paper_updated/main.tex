\begin{abstract}
Neural cellular automata (NCAs) are an appealing template for learned PDE emulators: one
small local rule, shared by every cell, mirrors the locality of a differential operator.
Used as-is, however, an NCA predicts free state increments, and on our benchmark it
drifts in mass by tens of units and on Navier--Stokes is worse than predicting no change
at all. We introduce the \emph{Physics-Informed Neural Cellular Automaton} (PI-NCA), in
which the shared rule outputs a flux instead of an increment and the state is advanced
by its periodic discrete divergence. Conservation then holds exactly for every value of
the weights, without a penalty term or a post-hoc correction. MC-PI-NCA extends the rule
to coupled systems by conserving each field separately, and a headroom-proportional
projection lets PI-NCA respect hard state bounds while keeping mass exact. We evaluate
the family against the Fourier neural operator (FNO), ResNet and U-Net surrogates at full
and matched size, the standard NCA, and an identity floor, on ten 2-D phenomena with five
to ten seeds and Holm-corrected paired tests. PI-NCA is the most accurate of fourteen
architectures on the shallow-water equations with $112\times$ fewer parameters than the
FNO; bounded PI-NCA variants take the top two places on Cahn--Hilliard, where the FNO is
twelfth ($0.357$ against $0.650$); and a PI-NCA with a spectral correction is best on
Allen--Cahn. Against the NCA it is built from, PI-NCA is better on seven of ten
phenomena with a third fewer parameters, and on heat it runs $3.4\times$ faster per
step than the FNO. Controlled ablations show why, and also where the prior should not be used.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
A learned emulator is useful only if it keeps producing plausible states once its own
errors are fed back as input. For physical systems, plausibility has a sharper meaning:
the quantities the governing equation conserves should stay conserved. Most neural
surrogates do not guarantee this. Global neural operators such as the FNO
\citep{li2020fno} learn accurate short-horizon maps, but nothing in their update prevents
total mass or momentum from wandering, and on every conservative phenomenon we measured
their end-of-rollout mass error is of order one or larger.

Neural cellular automata \citep{mordvintsev2020growing} look like a better starting point.
Their update is a single local rule applied identically at every cell, which is exactly
the structure of an explicit finite-difference stencil, and a useful rule fits in a few
thousand parameters. Recent work has shown that NCAs can be trained as autoregressive
PDE surrogates \citep{richardson2024patterns,saha2026timestepping}. The standard NCA
update, however, adds an unconstrained increment $f_\theta(s_t)$ to the state. That
increment is free to create or destroy any quantity, and the consequence is visible in
our benchmark: the standard NCA's heat error grows $22.9\times$ over the evaluation
horizon, its mass drifts by $65$ units, and on Navier--Stokes it scores below a
do-nothing identity emulator at ten seeds.

\paragraph{Idea.} We change \emph{what the rule predicts}. A PI-NCA cell emits a flux
along each spatial axis, and the state is updated by the periodic discrete divergence of
that flux, as in a finite-volume scheme \citep{leveque2002finite}. Because every unit of
flux leaving one cell enters its neighbour, the divergence sums to zero over the grid and
the total of each conserved field cannot change. This holds at initialization, during
training, and after training, for any weights. The rule stays as small and as local as
the NCA it replaces.

Two extensions make the construction usable beyond a single scalar field.
\textbf{MC-PI-NCA} handles coupled systems by predicting a separate flux pair for every
field, so height and each momentum component of the shallow-water equations are conserved
independently rather than as one lumped sum that would let one field borrow from another.
\textbf{Admissible-state projection} handles fields with hard physical ranges. Clipping
to the range breaks conservation, and the obvious uniform correction breaks the bound
again; we distribute the mass deficit in proportion to each cell's remaining headroom,
which restores mass exactly and cannot push any cell outside the range.

\paragraph{Evidence.} We compare the PI-NCA family with thirteen other emulators under
one training protocol, using an identity floor, physics-free ResNet and U-Net controls at
full and matched parameter counts, and the reference solver's own discretization error
to calibrate what the numbers can mean. Every comparison is a paired test on shared
held-out initial conditions with Holm--Bonferroni correction. The main findings are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Accuracy where the physics is conservative.} PI-NCA is first of fourteen on
the shallow-water equations ($5{,}280$ parameters, against the FNO's $592{,}995$), and
MC-PI-NCA is statistically tied with it and significantly better than both the FNO and
the U-Net. On advection--diffusion PI-NCA is second only to the FNO, and the gap between
them is eighteen times smaller than the solver's own discretization error.
\item \textbf{Stiff bounded dynamics.} With the admissible-state projection, PI-NCA
variants take first and second place on Cahn--Hilliard at ten seeds; the FNO is twelfth.
\item \textbf{A consistent improvement on the NCA.} PI-NCA beats the standard NCA on seven of
ten phenomena with a third fewer parameters, ties on one, and loses only on the two
reaction systems that do not conserve their fields.
\item \textbf{Cost.} PI-NCA trains $8.3\times$ faster and infers $3.4\times$ faster per
step than the FNO on heat, and its mass error is four orders of magnitude lower.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{PI-NCA}, a neural cellular automaton whose shared local rule predicts
fluxes, with exact per-step conservation for all parameters
(Section~\ref{sec:method}, Proposition~\ref{prop:conservation}).
\item \textbf{MC-PI-NCA}, the per-field extension to coupled multi-field systems, and a
\textbf{headroom projection} that makes the update simultaneously bound-preserving and
mass-exact whenever the target mass is feasible (Propositions~\ref{prop:perfield}
and~\ref{prop:headroom}).
\item \textbf{A controlled evaluation} on ten 2-D phenomena, with matched-capacity
ablations that isolate the conservation head, the perception reach, the bound, and the
choice of projection, and an explicit account of the regimes where the conservation
prior is the wrong one (Sections~\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:scaling}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Neural cellular automata for dynamics.}
NCAs learn one homogeneous local update applied at every cell
\citep{mordvintsev2020growing}. \citet{richardson2024patterns} train NCAs on PDE
trajectories, show generalization beyond the training data, and constrain them to respect
symmetries. \citet{saha2026timestepping} use an NCA surrogate for PDE time-stepping and
compare it with PDE-Net, a PINN and an FNO on five canonical equations. Together they
establish that NCAs are viable autoregressive surrogates. Our focus is different: we make
a conservation law a structural property of the rule itself, and ask what that buys.

\paragraph{Conservation by construction.}
Finite-volume bookkeeping makes conservation a property of the update rather than of the
loss \citep{leveque2002finite}. FINN brings this structure into learned models, composing
modules that represent inter-cell fluxes so each quantity obeys its own balance law
\citep{praditia2021finn,karlbauer2022composing}, and Neural Conservation Laws construct
networks that satisfy the continuity equation exactly \citep{richterpowell2022conservation}.
Other approaches enforce constraints by projection or correction after prediction
\citep{hansen2023probconserv,liu2024clawno}. We build directly on the flux-form idea and
do not claim it as new. PI-NCA differs in where the structure lives: the flux is produced
by a single shared cellular-automaton rule that is applied unchanged across all ten
equations we study, it is extended to per-field conservation for coupled systems, and it
is paired with a projection that keeps conservation exact under hard state bounds.

\paragraph{Learned discretizations and graph solvers.}
PDE-Net constrains convolution filters to approximate differential operators
\citep{long2018pdenet}, and data-driven discretizations learn stencil coefficients
\citep{barsinai2019discretizations}. Message-passing and mesh-based simulators generalize
across geometries and resolutions \citep{brandstetter2022message,pfaff2021mesh,%
sanchez2020simulation}, at the price of graph construction that a periodic regular grid
does not need, and with known under-reaching failure modes
\citep{tesan2025underreaching}. Our domains are periodic and regular, so these methods are
discussed rather than benchmarked.

\paragraph{Neural operators and physics-informed learning.}
The FNO learns a global operator through truncated Fourier mixing
\citep{li2020fno,neuraloperator}; DeepONet learns maps between function spaces
\citep{lu2021deeponet}; PINO and physics-informed DeepONet add residual terms
\citep{li2021pino,wang2021pideeponet}. PINNs fit one initial-value problem per run
\citep{raissi2019pinn} and have documented optimization and spectral-bias pathologies
\citep{krishnapriyan2021failure,wang2024causality,rahaman2019spectral}. We use the FNO as
the principal global baseline and compare against a PINN on a matched task in
Appendix~\ref{sec:operators}. Autoregressive rollout error is a distinct failure mode from
one-step accuracy \citep{lippe2023refiner,um2020solver}, which is why all models here are
trained through the rollout. Weak baselines and reporting bias are recognized problems
in scientific machine learning benchmarks \citep{mcgreivy2024baselines,pdebench,apebench},
which is why we report a trivial floor, physics-free controls and the solver's own error
next to every result.

\section{PI-NCA}
\label{sec:method}
\label{sec:methods}
\label{sec:arch}
Let $s_t\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times C}$ be the discrete state on a periodic grid, with
one channel per physical field. An emulator $g_\theta$ approximates the one-step map of a
numerical reference solver $\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}$ and is applied recursively.

\subsection{From state increments to fluxes}
A standard NCA perceives each cell's neighbourhood with a learned circular convolution
$P_\theta$, processes the percept with a per-cell MLP, and adds the result to the state:
\begin{equation}
 s_{t+1}=s_t+f_\theta(s_t),\qquad
 f_\theta(s_t)=\operatorname{MLP}_\theta\bigl(\operatorname{ReLU}(P_\theta(s_t))\bigr).
\label{eq:plain}
\end{equation}
PI-NCA keeps the perception and the MLP but reinterprets the output. The rule emits a flux
$F_\theta(s_t)=(F_1,\dots,F_d)$, one component per spatial axis, and the state advances by
its periodic backward-difference divergence:
\begin{equation}
 s_{t+1}=s_t+D_hF_\theta(s_t),\qquad
 D_hF=\sum_{a=1}^{d}\bigl(\operatorname{roll}_a(F_a)-F_a\bigr),
\label{eq:flux}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{roll}_a$ shifts a field by one cell along axis $a$ with wrap-around.
The quantity $F_a$ at a cell is the amount transferred across that cell's face on axis
$a$; the same amount is subtracted on one side and added on the other.

\begin{proposition}[Exact conservation]
\label{prop:conservation}
For every parameter value $\theta$ and every state $s_t$, the update of
Equation~\ref{eq:flux} satisfies $\sum_{\mathrm{cells}}s_{t+1}=\sum_{\mathrm{cells}}s_t$.
\end{proposition}
The proof is one line: $\operatorname{roll}_a$ permutes the cells, so
$\sum\operatorname{roll}_a(F_a)=\sum F_a$ and each term of $D_hF$ sums to zero
(Appendix~\ref{sec:proofs}). Two consequences matter in practice. First, conservation is
not something the optimizer has to discover or maintain, so it cannot be traded against
accuracy during training and cannot degrade over a long rollout. Second, the hypothesis
class is smaller: of all local updates, PI-NCA can only represent those that
redistribute. That restriction is the source of its advantage on conservative dynamics
and of its failure on reaction systems, and Section~\ref{sec:ablation} measures both. In
floating point the invariant holds to round-off, which on our grids is about $10^{-7}$
relative per rollout. The flux head is zero-initialized, so PI-NCA starts as the identity
map. The construction extends to three dimensions by adding the third axis to the sum.

\subsection{MC-PI-NCA: conservation for coupled fields}
For a system with $C$ fields, the rule predicts a flux pair for each field, $2C$ output
channels in 2-D, and applies the divergence channel by channel:
\begin{equation}
 s_{t+1}^{(c)}=s_t^{(c)}+D_hF^{(c)}_\theta(s_t),\qquad c=1,\dots,C,
\label{eq:mc}
\end{equation}
where every $F^{(c)}$ is computed from the full multi-field neighbourhood, so the fields
remain coupled through perception while their totals are held separately.

\begin{proposition}[Per-field conservation]
\label{prop:perfield}
Under Equation~\ref{eq:mc}, $\sum_{\mathrm{cells}}s^{(c)}_{t+1}=\sum_{\mathrm{cells}}
s^{(c)}_t$ for each field $c$ separately.
\end{proposition}
The distinction from conserving a single total is not cosmetic. A lumped constraint is
satisfied by an update that adds mass to one field and removes the same amount from
another, which is a violation for any system whose fields are distinct conserved
quantities; in the shallow-water equations it would allow fluid height to be converted
into momentum. MC-PI-NCA is the right prior for source-free conservation laws written in
conserved variables. In our experiments the compact PI-NCA configuration is applied to
multi-field phenomena through this same per-field construction, and MC-PI-NCA denotes
the configuration with wider perception and hidden layers ($48/96$ rather than $32/64$
channels) intended for coupled systems. On a single field the two differ only in width.

\subsection{Admissible states: bounds without losing mass}
Phase-field models such as Cahn--Hilliard evolve an order parameter confined to a range
$[\ell,h]$. Emulators that leave the range can diverge within a few steps, and clipping to
the range is an effective stabilizer. Clipping, though, destroys conservation. PI-NCA-B
therefore clips the candidate state and restores the recorded mass
$M_t=\sum s_t$:
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde s=\operatorname{clip}\bigl(s_t+D_hF_\theta(s_t),\ell,h\bigr),
 \qquad s_{t+1}=\mathcal{R}_{M_t}(\widetilde s).
\label{eq:bounded}
\end{equation}
The natural restoration $\mathcal{R}$, a uniform offset
$(M_t-\sum\widetilde s)/N$ added to all $N$ cells, moves cells that the clip has just
placed on a bound back outside it. We instead distribute the deficit
$d=M_t-\sum_j\widetilde s_j$ in proportion to each cell's headroom, $r_i=h-\widetilde s_i$
when mass must be added and $r_i=\widetilde s_i-\ell$ when it must be removed:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{R}_{M_t}(\widetilde s)_i=\widetilde s_i
 +\operatorname{sign}(d)\,\min\!\Bigl(\tfrac{|d|}{C_r},1\Bigr)\,r_i,
 \qquad C_r=\textstyle\sum_j r_j.
\label{eq:headroom}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Bounded and mass-exact]
\label{prop:headroom}
If $\widetilde s\in[\ell,h]^N$ and $M_t\in[N\ell,Nh]$, then
$\mathcal{R}_{M_t}(\widetilde s)\in[\ell,h]^N$ and $\sum\mathcal{R}_{M_t}(\widetilde s)=M_t$.
If $M_t$ lies outside that interval, the output stays in $[\ell,h]^N$ and saturates at
the nearest bound.
\end{proposition}
The map is a single vectorized pass and is differentiable almost everywhere, so it
trains end to end. The range $[\ell,h]$ is measured from a short solver rollout rather
than fixed by hand. Ablation A7 compares it with clip-only and uniform restoration.

\subsection{Reach and the PI-NCA family}
A local rule moves information about one cell per step, which limits it on dynamics
with long-range coupling. We examine two ways to extend reach on top of the same
conservative core. \textbf{PI-NCA-MS} concatenates perception features at dilations 1, 2
and 4 \citep{yu2016dilated}, reaching four cells per step for $5{,}520$ parameters.
\textbf{PI-NCA-Spec} adds a global Fourier residual to the local flux update and applies
mass restoration to the sum, so the combined update remains conservative. Suffix -B
denotes the admissible-state projection. Table~\ref{tab:axes} in the appendix summarizes
the structural properties of each member and of the baselines, and
Appendix~\ref{sec:architecture-details} gives layer-level details.

\paragraph{Training.} All models, PI-NCA and baselines alike, minimize the rollout loss
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_K(\theta)=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{\tau=1}^{K}
\operatorname{MSE}\!\left(g_\theta^{\circ\tau}(s_0),
\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\circ\tau}(s_0)\right),
\label{eq:training}
\end{equation}
with gradients propagated through the model's own trajectory. No term of the loss refers
to conservation; for the PI-NCA family it is already guaranteed.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:problem}
\label{sec:protocol}
\paragraph{Phenomena.} Ten 2-D periodic problems spanning the structural cases the prior
should distinguish: conservative scalar transport (heat, advection--diffusion), a
conservative multi-field law (shallow water), a stiff fourth-order bounded equation
(Cahn--Hilliard), a bounded reaction--diffusion equation (Allen--Cahn), a dispersive
system (wave), globally coupled flow (Navier--Stokes in vorticity form), and three
reaction systems whose fields are created and destroyed (Nagumo, FitzHugh--Nagumo,
Gray--Scott). Equations and teacher settings are in Appendix~\ref{sec:equations}.

\paragraph{Baselines.} The FNO \citep{li2020fno} at its standard size and at the NCA
parameter budget; a residual CNN and a U-Net, each at full size and matched to between
$5{,}364$ and $7{,}464$ parameters; the standard NCA of Equation~\ref{eq:plain}; and an
identity emulator $g(x)=x$ as a floor, which makes ``learned worse than nothing'' a
reportable outcome. All learned models have zero-initialized output heads and therefore
start as the identity. With the six PI-NCA configurations, this gives fourteen
architectures, each trained on every phenomenon.

\paragraph{Protocol.} One protocol for all 2-D results: a $48^2$ grid, batch 32, $K=12$
training steps, 48 evaluation steps, 1{,}200 epochs of AdamW with warmup, He
initialization, and teacher pre-evolution of training initial conditions (disabled on
Cahn--Hilliard, whose states coarsen too quickly for pre-evolved states to match
evaluation). Every architecture is trained with five seeds on every phenomenon, and with
ten seeds on heat, Cahn--Hilliard and Navier--Stokes. A single-seed 3-D suite is reported
in Appendix~\ref{sec:three-details}.

\paragraph{Statistics.} All models are evaluated on the same held-out initial conditions
in the same order, so comparisons are paired. The primary metric is per-initial-condition
relative $L^2$ error at the end of the rollout, summarized with a 10{,}000-sample
percentile bootstrap. A difference is reported as significant only when the bootstrap
interval of the paired differences excludes zero and a Wilcoxon signed-rank test rejects
after Holm--Bonferroni correction over the comparison family. We also record per-field
mass error, error growth over the horizon, spectral error distribution, parameter count,
training time and inference latency (Appendix~\ref{sec:metrics}).

\paragraph{Calibration.} Emulators are trained and scored against the solver, so we also
measure the solver's own error, against closed-form solutions for the linear problems
and by timestep refinement otherwise (Table~\ref{tab:teacher}). Differences smaller than
this error measure agreement with a particular discretization rather than physical
accuracy.

\paragraph{Compute.} All results come from one unattended 22.2-hour run on a single NVIDIA
RTX 4090, preceded by a 245-test correctness gate. Each results file records the software
version, device and full configuration.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{PI-NCA against the NCA it replaces}
\label{sec:vsnca}
The most direct test of the idea holds everything fixed except the update rule.
Table~\ref{tab:vsnca} compares PI-NCA with the standard NCA on all ten phenomena.

\input{table-vsnca}

PI-NCA is significantly more accurate on seven phenomena, by a factor of $7.9$ on heat,
$4.0$ on advection--diffusion and $2.3$ on shallow water, while using $4{,}576$
parameters to the NCA's $6{,}784$. The improvement is largest where the dynamics are
conservative and smooth, and it persists on problems with more complex structure:
Cahn--Hilliard, wave, Gray--Scott and Navier--Stokes. The flux form also changes how
errors behave over time. On heat the NCA's error grows $22.9\times$ from the first quarter
of the horizon to the end, against $5.5\times$ for PI-NCA (Table~\ref{tab:heat20}), and on
Navier--Stokes at ten seeds the NCA falls below the identity floor ($0.528$ against
$0.513$) while PI-NCA stays clearly above it ($0.441$, $p=3.1\times10^{-14}$). In these
experiments, restricting the rule to redistribution acts as a strong stabilizer for
autoregressive rollout.

The two losses are informative rather than incidental. Nagumo and FitzHugh--Nagumo have
reaction terms that create and destroy their fields, and a rule that can only move mass
cannot represent them: on Nagumo PI-NCA stays at the identity floor, and on
FitzHugh--Nagumo its error is $0.99$. We return to this in
Section~\ref{sec:ablation}, where the same effect appears under a fully matched ablation.

\subsection{Where the PI-NCA family leads}
\label{sec:leads}
Table~\ref{tab:regime2d} places the best PI-NCA model among all fourteen architectures on
each phenomenon, and Table~\ref{tab:pinca} gives the paired tests behind the main claims.

\input{table-regime2d}

\paragraph{Conservation laws.} Shallow water is the equation the construction is
designed for: three conserved fields, no sources, and coupling between them. Here PI-NCA
is the most accurate architecture in the study at $0.00709$ with $5{,}280$ parameters.
The FNO, with $592{,}995$ parameters, is third at $0.00869$, and PI-NCA has lower error on
$85\%$ of initial conditions. MC-PI-NCA is statistically indistinguishable from PI-NCA
($p=0.63$) and beats the FNO on $95\%$ of initial conditions and the U-Net on $90\%$. Both
hold each field's mass to about $10^{-4}$ in absolute terms over the rollout, against
$2.1$ for the FNO.

\paragraph{Stiff, bounded dynamics.} Cahn--Hilliard is fourth order and stiff, and its
order parameter is confined to a range. The two bounded PI-NCA variants take first and
second place at ten seeds: PI-NCA-MS-B at $0.357$ and PI-NCA-B at $0.368$. The strongest
model outside the family is the full-size ResNet at $0.465$, and the FNO is twelfth of
fourteen at $0.650$, beaten by PI-NCA-B on every one of the 80 paired initial conditions.
The solver's own error on this problem is $0.044$, so these differences are well above the
calibration level. The same ordering holds in the independent five-seed matrix.

\input{table-pinca}

\paragraph{Sharp interfaces with global structure.} On Allen--Cahn the best model is
PI-NCA-Spec, which pairs the conservative local update with a global spectral correction.
Its error of $0.00382$ is $2.3$ times lower than the FNO's $0.00889$, with $4.4$ times
fewer parameters, and it wins on every initial condition. On Navier--Stokes the same
hybrid is second to the FNO ($0.0826$ against $0.0730$).

\paragraph{Accuracy per parameter.} On heat the FNO remains the most accurate model.
PI-NCA-MS is third of fourteen at $0.00370$ with ten seeds, and the only two models ahead
of it are the FNO and PI-NCA-Spec, with $107$ and $24$ times as many parameters. At
$5{,}520$ parameters PI-NCA-MS is significantly more accurate than the U-Net, which is
$48$ times larger, and than the ResNet. On advection--diffusion PI-NCA is second with $4{,}576$
parameters, $2.3\times$ more accurate than the U-Net and $3.1\times$ more accurate than
the ResNet. Its gap to the FNO there, $2.7\times10^{-4}$, is eighteen times smaller than
the solver's own discretization error of $0.0047$, and on heat the FNO's error is
already within $30\%$ of the solver's. On these two problems the leading models have
reached the limit of what distillation from this solver can resolve, and within that
limit PI-NCA delivers comparable accuracy at two orders of magnitude fewer parameters.
Table~\ref{tab:eff} in the appendix groups all heat results by parameter budget.

\subsection{Exact conservation in practice}
Proposition~\ref{prop:conservation} is a statement about exact arithmetic; the question
for practice is what it buys in a trained model over a long rollout. On heat, the
absolute mass error at the end of the 48-step evaluation is $8.2\times10^{-5}$ for PI-NCA,
$2.04$ for the FNO, $2.74$ for the U-Net and $65.3$ for the standard NCA
(Table~\ref{tab:heat20}). The same separation holds on advection--diffusion, wave and
shallow water: every PI-NCA model stays below $10^{-3}$, and every learned model outside
the family, including the parameter-matched controls, is above $1$. The one setting in
which an unbounded PI-NCA loses mass is when an external clamp intervenes, which is
the Cahn--Hilliard case discussed under A1 below. For a conservative system this is not a
secondary metric. A surrogate whose total mass drifts produces states that are
physically inconsistent even when their pointwise error is small, and the drift
compounds with horizon.

\subsection{Why it works: controlled ablations}
\label{sec:ablation}
The comparisons above differ in more than one component. Table~\ref{tab:ablsummary}
isolates the mechanisms, changing exactly one element at matched backbone width.

\input{table-ablsummary}

\paragraph{A4: the conservation head.} Replacing a free residual head with the PI-NCA
flux head, with parameter counts within $1\%$, improves heat by $4.6\times$ ($0.00743$
against $0.0339$). On Nagumo the same substitution makes the error $24\times$ worse
($0.379$ against $0.0161$). The mass column explains both results. The flux head holds
the total below $10^{-4}$ on both equations; on heat that is the correct physics, and on
Nagumo, whose reaction term changes the total by design, it is precisely the wrong
constraint. The prior is powerful because it is strong, and it should be applied where
the target has the invariant.

\paragraph{A5: reach.} Widening perception from $3\times3$ to $5\times5$ to dilated
$(1,2,4)$ improves heat monotonically ($0.00896\to0.00584\to0.00412$) and Navier--Stokes
likewise ($0.679\to0.667\to0.583$). The local model's disadvantage on globally coupled
flow is a receptive-field limitation that can be reduced without abandoning locality or
conservation, which is the motivation for PI-NCA-MS.

\paragraph{A1 and A7: bounds and projection.} On Cahn--Hilliard, adding the
admissible-state projection to an identical $5{,}520$-parameter PI-NCA-MS backbone reduces
error from $0.515$ to $0.358$ and mass drift from $19.9$ to $5.2\times10^{-5}$; on
Allen--Cahn, whose states stay inside the range, it changes nothing. The drift of the
unbounded model does not contradict Proposition~\ref{prop:conservation}: every model in
the evaluation passes through a divergence guard that clamps states to $1.25$ times the
solver's physical range, and on Cahn--Hilliard the unbounded rollouts reach that clamp,
which removes mass the flux update had conserved. The projection is what makes bounding
and conservation compatible. A7 separates the two
parts of the projection. Clip-only, uniform restoration and headroom restoration have the
same accuracy ($0.3589$, $0.3614$, $0.3583$), but their mass drifts are $18.6$,
$1.07\times10^{-4}$ and $5.40\times10^{-5}$. The clip supplies stability, restoration
supplies conservation, and the headroom form of Equation~\ref{eq:headroom} halves the
remaining drift while being the only one that also guarantees the bound.

\subsection{Cost}
\label{sec:efficiency}
PI-NCA is inexpensive to train and to run. On heat it trains in $12.9$\,s against
$107.6$\,s for the FNO and infers in $1.23$\,ms per step at batch 8 against $4.17$\,ms, on
the same GPU. Its cost scales linearly with the number of cells, with no Fourier
transform. Against a physics-informed neural network on a matched task, PI-NCA-MS is more
accurate on all 32 held-out initial conditions ($0.0153$ against $0.1235$) while using
$431\times$ less total compute, because it trains once rather than once per initial
condition (Table~\ref{tab:matched}). We report the solver in the same comparison: at the
$32^2$ grid of that test it computes the exact reference in $19$\,ms per initial
condition, against $55$\,ms for the emulator. As for any learned surrogate, the
computational case for PI-NCA lies at larger grids, longer horizons and in
differentiable pipelines, not at the small grids used to benchmark it.

\subsection{Where PI-NCA is not the right model}
\label{sec:scaling}
\label{sec:teachererr}
\label{sec:matched}
\label{sec:spectral}
\label{sec:threed}
Three regimes fall outside the design. \textbf{Non-conservative reactions}: on Nagumo and
FitzHugh--Nagumo every PI-NCA model is at or near the identity floor, and A4 attributes
this directly to the conservation constraint. \textbf{Reaction--diffusion with physics-free
winners}: on Gray--Scott, wave and Nagumo a U-Net is best, and on Gray--Scott the best
PI-NCA model is sixth. \textbf{Global coupling and smooth transport}: on Navier--Stokes,
heat and advection--diffusion the FNO leads, although PI-NCA-Spec is close on the first
and PI-NCA is within the solver's error on the last.

Rankings also depend on scale. We varied grid, training horizon and training budget
around the main operating point for six representative architectures
(Table~\ref{tab:scaling}). On heat the winner is PI-NCA-MS at a $24^2$ grid and the FNO
at $48^2$: a local rule's reach is fixed in cells, so its relative advantage shrinks as
the domain grows. On Cahn--Hilliard the ordering changes along every axis, but at all
nine settings the best of the six swept architectures is a PI-NCA model. We therefore report every
ranking with its operating point.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
\label{sec:limitations}
PI-NCA turns a physical invariant into an architectural fact. The practical guidance
that follows from the results is short. If the target conserves its fields, a
flux-predicting rule is a better default than a free increment, and it is competitive
with, or better than, models two orders of magnitude larger. If the fields are coupled,
conserve them separately. If the state is bounded, restore mass by headroom rather than
uniformly. If the dynamics create or destroy the quantity, do not use the constraint.

Several limitations bound these conclusions. \textbf{Three evaluation axes are not
measured at this scale}: the out-of-distribution study, the long-horizon stability study
with the divergence guard disabled, and resolution transfer did not complete in the
GPU run, and we do not substitute reduced-scale results from other hardware
(Appendix~\ref{sec:notmeasured}); in particular we make no claim about PI-NCA's
robustness beyond the evaluation horizon or across resolutions. \textbf{Scope}: all domains
are periodic and regular, so Proposition~\ref{prop:conservation} as stated does not cover
non-periodic boundaries, which would need boundary fluxes. \textbf{Calibration}: on heat and
advection--diffusion the leading models have reached the solver's discretization error,
so small differences there are not measurements of physical accuracy. \textbf{3-D}: the
3-D suite is single-seed, lacks the physics-free controls, and does not include the
shallow-water or Cahn--Hilliard problems where PI-NCA is strongest; in it the FNO and
the standard NCA lead. \textbf{Baselines}: PINO is not evaluated, and our solvers are our
own, so numbers are not directly comparable to published PDEBench or APEBench results.
Finally, the headroom projection guarantees bounds and mass only when the target mass is
feasible for the range; otherwise the residual is reported rather than hidden.

\section{Conclusion}
We introduced PI-NCA, a neural cellular automaton whose shared local rule predicts fluxes
and therefore conserves each field exactly, for any weights. With its multi-field
extension and a bound-preserving mass projection, the PI-NCA family is the most accurate
of fourteen architectures on shallow water, Cahn--Hilliard and Allen--Cahn, is first or
second on six of ten phenomena, improves on the standard NCA on seven, and does so with a
few thousand parameters and a fraction of the FNO's training and inference cost. The
ablations show that the gains come from the conservation structure itself, and that the
same structure is the wrong choice for dynamics that do not conserve. Making physical
structure a property of the update, rather than a target for the optimizer, is a simple
change with a large effect, and cellular automata are a natural place to make it.

\section*{Ethics Statement}
This work concerns numerical PDE benchmarks and involves no human subjects. The main
foreseeable risk is treating a neural surrogate as a validated simulator. We therefore
distinguish solver emulation from continuum accuracy, report the solver's own error and
cost beside every model, and state which evaluation axes were not measured. Use in
safety-critical simulation would require application-specific validation beyond this
study.
