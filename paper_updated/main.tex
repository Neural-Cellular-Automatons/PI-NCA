\begin{abstract}
Neural cellular automata (NCAs) are an appealing template for learned PDE emulators: one
small local rule, shared by every cell, mirrors the locality of a differential operator.
Used as-is, however, an NCA predicts free state increments; on our benchmark it drifts in
mass by tens of units and on Navier--Stokes is worse than predicting no change at all. We
introduce two architectures that remove this failure at its source. The
\emph{Physics-Informed Neural Cellular Automaton} (PI-NCA) makes the shared rule output a
flux rather than an increment and advances the state by the flux's periodic discrete
divergence, so the total of the field is conserved exactly for every value of the
weights. \emph{MC-PI-NCA} extends the construction to coupled systems, predicting one flux
per field and conserving each field's total independently. Either model can append a
parameter-free projection that enforces a hard state range while keeping mass exact. We
compare both with the Fourier neural operator (FNO), ResNet and U-Net surrogates at full
and matched size, the standard NCA and an identity floor, on ten 2-D phenomena with five
to ten seeds and Holm-corrected paired tests. On the shallow-water equations PI-NCA and
MC-PI-NCA are the two most accurate models, with $112\times$ and $54\times$ fewer
parameters than the FNO; on Cahn--Hilliard, PI-NCA with the projection is the most
accurate model ($0.368$ against $0.465$ for the best baseline and $0.650$ for the FNO);
on heat and advection--diffusion they are second only to the FNO with $60$--$130\times$
fewer parameters. PI-NCA improves on the standard NCA on seven of ten phenomena with a
third fewer parameters, and trains $8.3\times$ faster than the FNO. Ablations isolate the
flux head and the projection, and identify where the conservation prior should not be
used.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
A learned emulator is useful only if it keeps producing plausible states once its own
errors are fed back as input. For physical systems, plausibility has a sharper meaning:
the quantities the governing equation conserves should stay conserved. Most neural
surrogates do not guarantee this. Global neural operators such as the FNO
\citep{li2020fno} learn accurate short-horizon maps, but nothing in their update prevents
total mass or momentum from wandering, and on every conservative phenomenon we measured
their end-of-rollout mass error is of order one or larger.

Neural cellular automata \citep{mordvintsev2020growing} look like a better starting point.
Their update is a single local rule applied identically at every cell, which is exactly
the structure of an explicit finite-difference stencil, and a useful rule fits in a few
thousand parameters. Recent work has shown that NCAs can be trained as autoregressive PDE
surrogates \citep{richardson2024patterns,saha2026timestepping}. The standard NCA update,
however, adds an unconstrained increment $f_\theta(s_t)$ to the state. That increment is
free to create or destroy any quantity, and the consequence is visible in our benchmark:
the standard NCA's heat error grows $22.9\times$ over the evaluation horizon, its total
heat drifts by $65$ units, and on Navier--Stokes it scores below a do-nothing identity
emulator at ten seeds.

\paragraph{PI-NCA.} We change \emph{what the rule predicts}. A PI-NCA cell emits a flux
along each spatial axis, and the state is updated by the periodic discrete divergence of
that flux, as in a finite-volume scheme \citep{leveque2002finite}. Every unit of flux
leaving one cell enters its neighbour, so the divergence sums to zero over the grid and
the total cannot change. This holds at initialization, during training and after
training, for any weights, and the rule stays as small and as local as the NCA it
replaces.

\paragraph{MC-PI-NCA.} Physical systems usually carry several conserved fields that
interact, such as fluid height and the two momentum components of the shallow-water
equations. A single conserved sum is the wrong constraint for them, because it allows
one field to gain what another loses. MC-PI-NCA perceives all fields jointly but predicts a
separate flux pair for each, so every field's total is conserved independently while the
fields remain coupled through the shared perception.

Both architectures can also respect a hard state range, such as the order parameter of a
phase-field model. Clipping to the range breaks conservation, and the obvious uniform
correction breaks the range again. We restore mass in proportion to each cell's remaining
headroom, which is exact, cannot push a cell outside the range, and adds no parameters.

\paragraph{Results in brief.} We compare the two architectures with seven learned baselines
and an identity floor under one training protocol, using paired tests on shared held-out
initial conditions with Holm--Bonferroni correction, and calibrate every result against
the reference solver's own discretization error.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Coupled conservation laws.} On shallow water, PI-NCA ($5{,}280$ parameters)
and MC-PI-NCA ($10{,}992$) are the two most accurate of eleven models. Both are
significantly more accurate than the FNO ($592{,}995$ parameters), and MC-PI-NCA also
than the U-Net.
\item \textbf{Stiff bounded dynamics.} On Cahn--Hilliard, PI-NCA with the bound projection
is the most accurate model at ten seeds, better than the FNO on every one of 80 initial
conditions. The projection alone lowers PI-NCA's error by $30\%$.
\item \textbf{Accuracy per parameter.} On heat MC-PI-NCA, and on advection--diffusion
PI-NCA, are second only to an FNO $60$ and $130$ times larger; MC-PI-NCA is significantly
better than the ResNet on heat, and PI-NCA than both the ResNet and the U-Net on
advection--diffusion.
\item \textbf{Over the standard NCA.} PI-NCA is more accurate on seven of ten phenomena
with a third fewer parameters, and loses only on the two reaction systems that do not
conserve their fields.
\item \textbf{Cost and consistency.} On heat, PI-NCA trains $8.3\times$ and infers
$3.4\times$ faster per step than the FNO, and its mass error is four orders of magnitude
lower.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{PI-NCA}, a neural cellular automaton whose shared local rule predicts
fluxes, with exact conservation for all parameters (Proposition~\ref{prop:conservation}).
\item \textbf{MC-PI-NCA}, its extension to coupled multi-field systems with independent
per-field conservation (Proposition~\ref{prop:perfield}), together with a parameter-free
bound projection that keeps mass exact under a hard state range
(Proposition~\ref{prop:headroom}).
\item \textbf{A controlled evaluation} on ten 2-D phenomena with matched-capacity
ablations of the flux head and the projection, and an explicit account of the regimes
where the conservation prior is the wrong one.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Neural cellular automata for dynamics.}
NCAs learn one homogeneous local update applied at every cell
\citep{mordvintsev2020growing}. \citet{richardson2024patterns} train NCAs on PDE
trajectories, show generalization beyond the training data, and constrain them to respect
symmetries. \citet{saha2026timestepping} use an NCA surrogate for PDE time-stepping and
compare it with PDE-Net, a PINN and an FNO on five canonical equations. Together they
establish that NCAs are viable autoregressive surrogates. Our focus is different: we make
a conservation law a structural property of the rule itself, and ask what that buys.

\paragraph{Conservation by construction.}
Finite-volume bookkeeping makes conservation a property of the update rather than of the
loss \citep{leveque2002finite}. FINN brings this structure into learned models, composing
modules that represent inter-cell fluxes so that each quantity obeys its own balance law
\citep{praditia2021finn,karlbauer2022composing}, and Neural Conservation Laws construct
networks that satisfy the continuity equation exactly \citep{richterpowell2022conservation}.
Other approaches enforce constraints by projection or correction after prediction
\citep{hansen2023probconserv,liu2024clawno}. We build directly on the flux-form idea and
do not claim it as new. PI-NCA and MC-PI-NCA differ in where the structure lives: the flux
is produced by a single shared cellular-automaton rule that is applied unchanged across
all ten equations we study, conservation is held per field for coupled systems, and it is
kept exact under hard state bounds by a projection with a feasibility guarantee.

\paragraph{Learned discretizations and graph solvers.}
PDE-Net constrains convolution filters to approximate differential operators
\citep{long2018pdenet}, and data-driven discretizations learn stencil coefficients
\citep{barsinai2019discretizations}. Message-passing and mesh-based simulators generalize
across geometries and resolutions \citep{brandstetter2022message,pfaff2021mesh,%
sanchez2020simulation}, at the price of graph construction that a periodic regular grid
does not need, and with known under-reaching failure modes
\citep{tesan2025underreaching}. Our domains are periodic and regular, so these methods are
discussed rather than benchmarked.

\paragraph{Neural operators and physics-informed learning.}
The FNO learns a global operator through truncated Fourier mixing
\citep{li2020fno,neuraloperator}; DeepONet learns maps between function spaces
\citep{lu2021deeponet}; PINO and physics-informed DeepONet add residual terms
\citep{li2021pino,wang2021pideeponet}. PINNs fit one initial-value problem per run
\citep{raissi2019pinn} and have documented optimization and spectral-bias pathologies
\citep{krishnapriyan2021failure,wang2024causality,rahaman2019spectral}. We use the FNO as
the principal global baseline. Autoregressive rollout error is a distinct failure mode from
one-step accuracy \citep{lippe2023refiner,um2020solver}, which is why every model here is
trained through the rollout. Weak baselines and reporting bias are recognized problems in
scientific machine learning benchmarks \citep{mcgreivy2024baselines,pdebench,apebench},
which is why we report a trivial floor, physics-free controls and the solver's own error
next to every result.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\label{sec:methods}
\label{sec:arch}
Let $s_t\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times C}$ be the discrete state on a periodic grid, with
one channel per physical field. An emulator $g_\theta$ approximates the one-step map of a
numerical reference solver $\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}$ and is applied recursively.

\subsection{PI-NCA: from state increments to fluxes}
A standard NCA perceives each cell's neighbourhood with a learned circular convolution
$P_\theta$, processes the percept with a per-cell MLP, and adds the result to the state:
\begin{equation}
 s_{t+1}=s_t+f_\theta(s_t),\qquad
 f_\theta(s_t)=\operatorname{MLP}_\theta\bigl(\operatorname{ReLU}(P_\theta(s_t))\bigr).
\label{eq:plain}
\end{equation}
PI-NCA keeps the perception and the MLP but reinterprets the output. The rule emits a flux
$F_\theta(s_t)=(F_1,\dots,F_d)$, one component per spatial axis, and the state advances by
its periodic backward-difference divergence:
\begin{equation}
 s_{t+1}=s_t+D_hF_\theta(s_t),\qquad
 D_hF=\sum_{a=1}^{d}\bigl(\operatorname{roll}_a(F_a)-F_a\bigr),
\label{eq:flux}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{roll}_a$ shifts a field by one cell along axis $a$ with wrap-around.
$F_a$ at a cell is the amount transferred across that cell's face on axis $a$; the same
amount is subtracted on one side and added on the other. The network is a $3\times3$
circular perception convolution with 32 channels, a per-cell MLP with 64 and 32 channels,
and a zero-initialized flux head, $4{,}576$ parameters for a single field. Because the head
starts at zero, PI-NCA starts as the identity map.

\begin{proposition}[Exact conservation]
\label{prop:conservation}
For every parameter value $\theta$ and every state $s_t$, the update of
Equation~\ref{eq:flux} satisfies $\sum_{\mathrm{cells}}s_{t+1}=\sum_{\mathrm{cells}}s_t$.
\end{proposition}
The proof is one line: $\operatorname{roll}_a$ permutes the cells, so
$\sum\operatorname{roll}_a(F_a)=\sum F_a$ and each term of $D_hF$ sums to zero
(Appendix~\ref{sec:proofs}). Two consequences matter in practice. Conservation is not
something the optimizer has to discover or maintain, so it cannot be traded against
accuracy during training and cannot degrade over a long rollout. And the hypothesis class
is smaller: of all local updates, PI-NCA can represent only those that redistribute. That
restriction is the source of its advantage on conservative dynamics and of its failure on
reaction systems, and Section~\ref{sec:ablation} measures both. In floating point the
invariant holds to round-off, about $10^{-7}$ relative over a rollout on our grids. The
construction extends to three dimensions by adding the third axis to the sum.

\subsection{MC-PI-NCA: conservation for coupled fields}
For a system with $C$ fields, MC-PI-NCA predicts a flux pair for each field, $2C$ output
channels in 2-D, and applies the divergence channel by channel:
\begin{equation}
 s_{t+1}^{(c)}=s_t^{(c)}+D_hF^{(c)}_\theta(s_t),\qquad c=1,\dots,C.
\label{eq:mc}
\end{equation}
Every $F^{(c)}$ is computed from the full multi-field neighbourhood, so the fields remain
coupled through perception while their totals are held separately. MC-PI-NCA is sized for
this joint perception, with 48 perception and 96 hidden channels.

\begin{proposition}[Per-field conservation]
\label{prop:perfield}
Under Equation~\ref{eq:mc}, $\sum_{\mathrm{cells}}s^{(c)}_{t+1}=\sum_{\mathrm{cells}}
s^{(c)}_t$ for each field $c$ separately.
\end{proposition}
The distinction from conserving a single total is not cosmetic. A lumped constraint is
satisfied by an update that adds mass to one field and removes the same amount from
another, which violates the physics of any system whose fields are distinct conserved
quantities; in the shallow-water equations it would let fluid height be converted into
momentum. MC-PI-NCA is the natural prior for source-free conservation laws written in
conserved variables. When PI-NCA itself is applied to a multi-field problem in our
experiments, it uses this same per-field divergence at its compact width, so on
single-field problems the two architectures differ only in width and the comparison
between them on coupled systems isolates the capacity of the joint perception.

\subsection{Bound projection: hard ranges without losing mass}
Phase-field models such as Cahn--Hilliard evolve an order parameter confined to a range
$[\ell,h]$. Emulators that leave the range can diverge within a few steps, and clipping is
an effective stabilizer, but clipping destroys conservation. With the projection enabled,
PI-NCA clips the candidate state and restores the recorded mass $M_t=\sum s_t$:
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde s=\operatorname{clip}\bigl(s_t+D_hF_\theta(s_t),\ell,h\bigr),
 \qquad s_{t+1}=\mathcal{R}_{M_t}(\widetilde s).
\label{eq:bounded}
\end{equation}
The natural restoration, a uniform offset $(M_t-\sum\widetilde s)/N$ added to all $N$
cells, moves cells that the clip has just placed on a bound back outside it. We instead
distribute the deficit $d=M_t-\sum_j\widetilde s_j$ in proportion to each cell's headroom,
$r_i=h-\widetilde s_i$ when mass must be added and $r_i=\widetilde s_i-\ell$ when it must
be removed:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{R}_{M_t}(\widetilde s)_i=\widetilde s_i
 +\operatorname{sign}(d)\,\min\!\Bigl(\tfrac{|d|}{C_r},1\Bigr)\,r_i,
 \qquad C_r=\textstyle\sum_j r_j.
\label{eq:headroom}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Bounded and mass-exact]
\label{prop:headroom}
If $\widetilde s\in[\ell,h]^N$ and $M_t\in[N\ell,Nh]$, then
$\mathcal{R}_{M_t}(\widetilde s)\in[\ell,h]^N$ and $\sum\mathcal{R}_{M_t}(\widetilde s)=M_t$.
If $M_t$ lies outside that interval, the output stays in $[\ell,h]^N$ and saturates at
the nearest bound.
\end{proposition}
The projection is a single vectorized pass with no parameters, differentiable almost
everywhere, so it trains end to end. It is a configuration of the architecture rather than
a different model, intended only for fields that have a physical range; the range is
measured from a short solver rollout rather than fixed by hand. We refer to PI-NCA with
the projection enabled as \emph{bounded PI-NCA}.

\paragraph{Training.} Every model, proposed or baseline, minimizes the same rollout loss
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_K(\theta)=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{\tau=1}^{K}
\operatorname{MSE}\!\left(g_\theta^{\circ\tau}(s_0),
\mathcal{S}_{\Delta t}^{\circ\tau}(s_0)\right),
\label{eq:training}
\end{equation}
with gradients propagated through the model's own trajectory. No term of the loss refers
to conservation; for PI-NCA and MC-PI-NCA it is already guaranteed.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:problem}
\label{sec:protocol}
\paragraph{Phenomena.} Ten 2-D periodic problems spanning the structural cases the prior
should distinguish: conservative scalar transport (heat, advection--diffusion), a
conservative multi-field law (shallow water), a stiff fourth-order bounded equation
(Cahn--Hilliard), a bounded reaction--diffusion equation (Allen--Cahn), a dispersive
system (wave), globally coupled flow (Navier--Stokes in vorticity form), and three
reaction systems whose fields are created and destroyed (Nagumo, FitzHugh--Nagumo,
Gray--Scott). Equations and solver settings are in Appendix~\ref{sec:equations}.

\paragraph{Models.} The proposed models are PI-NCA, bounded PI-NCA and MC-PI-NCA. The
baselines are the FNO \citep{li2020fno} at its standard size and at the NCA parameter
budget (FNO-S); a residual CNN (ResNet) and a U-Net, each at full size and at a matched
budget of $5{,}364$--$7{,}464$ parameters (ResNet-S, U-Net-S); the standard NCA of
Equation~\ref{eq:plain}; and an identity emulator $g(x)=x$ as a floor, which makes
``learned worse than nothing'' a reportable outcome. All learned models have
zero-initialized output heads and start as the identity. The same run also trained
further exploratory conservative variants that are outside the scope of this paper; they
are not reported, and every rank we quote is among the eleven models listed here.

\paragraph{Protocol.} One protocol for all 2-D results: a $48^2$ grid, batch 32, $K=12$
training steps, 48 evaluation steps, 1{,}200 epochs of AdamW with warmup, He
initialization, and solver pre-evolution of training initial conditions (disabled on
Cahn--Hilliard, whose states coarsen too quickly for pre-evolved states to match
evaluation). Every model is trained with five seeds on every phenomenon, and with ten
seeds on heat, Cahn--Hilliard and Navier--Stokes. A single-seed 3-D suite is reported in
Appendix~\ref{sec:three-details}.

\paragraph{Statistics.} All models are evaluated on the same held-out initial conditions
in the same order, so comparisons are paired. The primary metric is per-initial-condition
relative $L^2$ error at the end of the rollout, summarized with a 10{,}000-sample
percentile bootstrap. A difference is reported as significant only when the bootstrap
interval of the paired differences excludes zero and a Wilcoxon signed-rank test rejects
after Holm--Bonferroni correction over the comparison family. We also record per-field
mass error, error growth over the horizon, spectral error distribution, parameter count,
training time and inference latency (Appendix~\ref{sec:metrics}).

\paragraph{Calibration.} Emulators are trained and scored against the solver, so we also
measure the solver's own error, against closed-form solutions for the linear problems and
by timestep refinement otherwise (Table~\ref{tab:teacher}). Differences smaller than this
error measure agreement with a particular discretization rather than physical accuracy.

\paragraph{Compute.} All results come from one unattended 22.2-hour run on a single NVIDIA
RTX 4090, preceded by a 233-test correctness gate. Each results file records the software
version, device and full configuration.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{PI-NCA against the NCA it replaces}
\label{sec:vsnca}
The most direct test of the idea holds everything fixed except what the rule predicts.
Table~\ref{tab:vsnca} compares PI-NCA with the standard NCA on all ten phenomena.

\input{table-vsnca}

PI-NCA is significantly more accurate on seven phenomena, by a factor of $7.9$ on heat,
$4.0$ on advection--diffusion and $2.3$ on shallow water, while using $4{,}576$
parameters to the NCA's $6{,}784$. The improvement is largest where the dynamics are
conservative and smooth, and it persists on problems with more complex structure:
Cahn--Hilliard, wave, Gray--Scott and Navier--Stokes. The flux form also changes how errors
evolve. On heat the NCA's error grows $22.9\times$ from the first quarter of the horizon to
the end, against $5.5\times$ for PI-NCA (Table~\ref{tab:heat20}), and on Navier--Stokes at
ten seeds the NCA falls below the identity floor ($0.528$ against $0.513$) while PI-NCA
stays clearly above it ($0.441$, $p=3.1\times10^{-14}$). In these experiments, restricting
the rule to redistribution acts as a strong stabilizer for autoregressive rollout.

The two losses are informative rather than incidental. Nagumo and FitzHugh--Nagumo have
reaction terms that create and destroy their fields, and a rule that can only move mass
cannot represent them: on Nagumo PI-NCA stays at the identity floor, and on
FitzHugh--Nagumo its error is $0.99$. Section~\ref{sec:ablation} shows the same effect under
a fully matched ablation.

\subsection{Where PI-NCA and MC-PI-NCA lead}
\label{sec:leads}
Table~\ref{tab:regime2d} places the better proposed model among all eleven models on each
phenomenon, and Table~\ref{tab:pinca} gives the paired tests behind the main claims.

\input{table-regime2d}

\paragraph{Coupled conservation laws.} Shallow water is the problem the construction is
built for: three conserved fields, no sources, and coupling between them. PI-NCA is the
most accurate model at $0.00709$ with $5{,}280$ parameters, and MC-PI-NCA is second at
$0.00742$, statistically indistinguishable from it ($p=0.63$). The FNO, with $592{,}995$
parameters, is third at $0.00869$. PI-NCA has lower error than the FNO on $85\%$ of initial
conditions, and MC-PI-NCA on $95\%$; MC-PI-NCA also beats the U-Net on $90\%$. Both proposed
models hold each field's mass to about $10^{-4}$ in absolute terms over the rollout, against
$2.1$ for the FNO and $3.0$ for the U-Net.

\input{table-pinca}

\paragraph{Stiff, bounded dynamics.} Cahn--Hilliard is fourth order and stiff, and its
order parameter is confined to a range. With the bound projection, PI-NCA is the most
accurate model at ten seeds ($0.368$). The strongest baseline is the full-size ResNet at
$0.465$, which bounded PI-NCA beats on $91\%$ of initial conditions with $16$ times fewer
parameters; the FNO is ninth of eleven at $0.650$ and loses on all 80. The solver's own
error on this problem is $0.044$, so these differences are well above the calibration
level, and the independent five-seed matrix has the same leader and runner-up. Without
the projection, PI-NCA ($0.528$) has a lower mean error than the U-Net and the FNO but
trails the ResNet; the projection is what turns a competitive model into the best one
(Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\paragraph{Accuracy per parameter.} On heat and advection--diffusion the FNO is the most
accurate model, and a proposed model is second on both. On heat MC-PI-NCA reaches $0.00377$
with $9{,}936$ parameters, a sixtieth of the FNO's; it is significantly better than the
full-size ResNet and the matched ResNet-S, and statistically tied with a U-Net $27$ times
larger. On advection--diffusion PI-NCA reaches $0.00150$ with $4{,}576$ parameters, $130$
times fewer than the FNO, and is $2.3\times$ more accurate than the U-Net and $3.1\times$
more accurate than the ResNet. Both problems are close to the limit of what distillation
from this solver can resolve: the FNO's heat error is within $30\%$ of the solver's own
error, and on advection--diffusion PI-NCA's gap to the FNO, $2.7\times10^{-4}$, is eighteen
times smaller than the solver's error of $0.0047$. Within that limit the proposed models
deliver comparable accuracy at two orders of magnitude fewer parameters.
Table~\ref{tab:eff} in the appendix groups the heat results by parameter budget; MC-PI-NCA
is the most accurate model in the small budget.

\subsection{Exact conservation in practice}
Proposition~\ref{prop:conservation} is a statement about exact arithmetic; the practical
question is what it buys in a trained model over a long rollout. On heat, the absolute mass
error at the end of the 48-step evaluation is $8.2\times10^{-5}$ for PI-NCA and
$6.1\times10^{-5}$ for MC-PI-NCA, against $2.04$ for the FNO, $2.74$ for the U-Net and
$65.3$ for the standard NCA (Table~\ref{tab:heat20}). The same separation holds on
advection--diffusion, wave and shallow water: both proposed models stay below $10^{-3}$,
and every learned baseline, including the parameter-matched ones, is above $1$. For a
conservative system this is not a secondary metric. A surrogate whose total drifts
produces physically inconsistent states even when its pointwise error is small, and the
drift compounds with horizon. The one setting in which an unbounded PI-NCA loses mass is
when an external clamp intervenes, which is the Cahn--Hilliard case discussed under A1.

\subsection{Why it works: controlled ablations}
\label{sec:ablation}
The comparisons above differ in more than one component. Table~\ref{tab:ablsummary}
isolates the two design choices, changing exactly one element at a time.

\input{table-ablsummary}

\paragraph{A4: the flux head.} Replacing a free residual head with the PI-NCA flux head, at
a matched backbone and within $1\%$ of the parameter count, improves heat by $4.6\times$
($0.00743$ against $0.0339$). On Nagumo the same substitution makes the error $24\times$
worse ($0.379$ against $0.0161$). The mass column explains both. The flux head holds the
total below $10^{-4}$ on both equations; on heat that is the correct physics, and on Nagumo,
whose reaction term changes the total by design, it is precisely the wrong constraint. The
prior is powerful because it is strong, and it belongs where the target has the invariant.

\paragraph{A1: the bound projection.} On Cahn--Hilliard, enabling the projection on an
otherwise identical $4{,}576$-parameter PI-NCA lowers error from $0.528$ to $0.368$, better
on all 80 paired initial conditions, and lowers mass drift from $38.3$ to
$5.5\times10^{-5}$. The drift of the unprojected model does not contradict
Proposition~\ref{prop:conservation}: every model in the evaluation passes through a
divergence guard that clamps states to $1.25$ times the solver's physical range, and on
Cahn--Hilliard the unprojected rollouts reach that clamp, which removes mass the flux update
had conserved. The projection makes bounding and conservation compatible. Its cost
elsewhere is the expected one. On Allen--Cahn the error rises by $0.3\%$, small but
significant; on shallow water, whose dynamics have no hard bound, enforcing a measured
range more than doubles the error. The projection
should be enabled only for fields with a genuine physical range.

\subsection{Cost}
\label{sec:efficiency}
Both architectures are inexpensive to train and to run. On heat, PI-NCA trains in
$12.9$\,s and MC-PI-NCA in $18.2$\,s, against $107.6$\,s for the FNO, and they infer in
$1.22$ and $1.32$\,ms per step at batch 8 against $4.17$\,ms, on the same GPU. Their cost
scales linearly with the number of cells and involves no Fourier transform. As for any
learned surrogate, the computational case lies at larger grids, longer horizons and in
differentiable pipelines rather than at the small grids used to benchmark it; at $48^2$ the
reference solver itself costs $1.2$--$4.9$\,ms per step (Table~\ref{tab:teacher}).

\subsection{Where the conservation prior does not fit}
\label{sec:scaling}
\label{sec:teachererr}
\label{sec:spectral}
\label{sec:threed}
Three regimes fall outside the design. \textbf{Non-conservative reactions}: on Nagumo,
FitzHugh--Nagumo and Gray--Scott the proposed models are far behind; on Gray--Scott they
are worse than the identity floor and on Nagumo level with it; A4 attributes this directly to the
conservation constraint. \textbf{Global coupling}: on Navier--Stokes the FNO and the U-Net
lead, and the best proposed model is sixth, although still above the floor where the
standard NCA is not. \textbf{Sharp interfaces with non-conserved dynamics}: on Allen--Cahn
the FNO leads and MC-PI-NCA is fifth. On wave PI-NCA is sixth, though within $5\%$ of the
best error.

The ranking is stable where the proposed models lead. We varied grid, training horizon and
training budget around the main operating point for five representative models
(Table~\ref{tab:scaling}). On Cahn--Hilliard PI-NCA is the best of the five at every one of
the nine settings, even without the projection; on heat and Navier--Stokes the FNO is best
at every setting. Orderings below the winner do change, most strongly on Navier--Stokes
along the training horizon ($\tau=0$), so every ranking is quoted with its operating point.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
\label{sec:limitations}
PI-NCA and MC-PI-NCA turn a physical invariant into an architectural fact. The guidance
that follows from the results is short. If the target conserves its fields, a
flux-predicting rule is a better default than a free increment, and it is competitive
with, or better than, models two orders of magnitude larger. If the fields are coupled,
conserve them separately. If the state has a hard range, enable the headroom projection;
if it does not, leave it off. If the dynamics create or destroy the quantity, do not use
the constraint.

Several limitations bound these conclusions. \textbf{Three evaluation axes are not
measured at this scale}: the out-of-distribution study, the long-horizon stability study
with the divergence guard disabled, and resolution transfer did not complete in the GPU
run, and we do not substitute reduced-scale results from other hardware
(Appendix~\ref{sec:notmeasured}); in particular we make no claim about robustness beyond
the evaluation horizon or across resolutions. \textbf{Scope}: all domains are periodic and
regular, so Proposition~\ref{prop:conservation} as stated does not cover non-periodic
boundaries, which would need boundary fluxes. \textbf{Calibration}: on heat and
advection--diffusion the leading models have reached the solver's discretization error, so
small differences there are not measurements of physical accuracy. \textbf{Reach}: a
$3\times3$ rule moves information one cell per step, which limits both architectures on
globally coupled flow; wider perception, for instance through dilated stencils
\citep{yu2016dilated}, is compatible with the flux construction and is outside the scope
of this paper. \textbf{Projection}: its advantage over the uniform correction is
established by Proposition~\ref{prop:headroom}, not by a separate experiment on these
architectures. \textbf{3-D}: the 3-D suite is single-seed, lacks the convolutional controls,
and does not include shallow water or Cahn--Hilliard, where the proposed models are
strongest; there the proposed models are second on heat and advection--diffusion.
\textbf{Baselines}: PINO is not evaluated, and our solvers are our own, so numbers are not
directly comparable with published PDEBench or APEBench results.

\section{Conclusion}
We introduced PI-NCA, a neural cellular automaton whose shared local rule predicts fluxes
and therefore conserves exactly for any weights, and MC-PI-NCA, which extends that
guarantee to each field of a coupled system. The two architectures are the most accurate
of eleven models on the shallow-water equations and, with a parameter-free bound
projection, on Cahn--Hilliard; they are second only to a far larger FNO on heat and
advection--diffusion; and PI-NCA improves on the standard NCA on seven of ten phenomena,
with a few thousand parameters and a fraction of the FNO's training and inference cost.
Ablations show that the gains come from the conservation structure itself, and that the
same structure is the wrong choice for dynamics that do not conserve. Making physical
structure a property of the update, rather than a target for the optimizer, is a simple
change with a large effect, and cellular automata are a natural place to make it.

\section*{Ethics Statement}
This work concerns numerical PDE benchmarks and involves no human subjects. The main
foreseeable risk is treating a neural surrogate as a validated simulator. We therefore
distinguish solver emulation from continuum accuracy, report the solver's own error and
cost beside every model, and state which evaluation axes were not measured. Use in
safety-critical simulation would require application-specific validation beyond this
study.
